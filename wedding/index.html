Taste of Portugal

What to eat & drink

You will not eat badly in Lisbon. But make sure these cross your table at least once.

Pastel de nata

Get one at Manteigaria and one at Pastéis de Belém — then argue about which is better.

Bacalhau

Portugal claims 100+ ways to cook its beloved salt cod. We won't judge your favorite.

Grilled fish & seafood

Fresh off the boat, simply grilled. Ramiro is the legend — book ahead.

Bifanas & piri-piri chicken

Casual, cheap, incredible. Best eaten standing at a counter.

Ginjinha

Sour-cherry liqueur, a Lisbon ritual. Order it com — with a cherry in the cup.

Vinho Verde & Douro reds

Ask your waiter to pick. You genuinely cannot go wrong.
Timing tip: July is peak sardine season — follow the smoke to charcoal grills in Alfama's lanes.

The practical stuff

Good to know

Everything your group chat was going to ask anyway.

Getting around

  • Uber and Bolt are everywhere, cheap, and the easiest way to move around.
  • Tram 15 runs out toward Belém; the classic 28 winds through Alfama.
  • The hotel shuttle can drop you in the center — ask the front desk for times.
  • Lisbon is hilly and cobbled — assume every walk has a climb in it.

Before you go

  • Currency is the euro. Cards are accepted almost everywhere; keep a little cash for kiosks and trams.
  • Outlets are European two-pin (types C/F), 230V — pack an adapter.
  • July is hot and sunny — high 80s°F days, breezy evenings by the river.
  • Tipping is modest here — rounding up or ~5–10% for great service is plenty.

Pack for this

  • Comfortable walking shoes — seriously, the cobblestones are no joke.
  • Formal, dressy attire for the wedding day.
  • A light layer for breezy evenings.
  • Swimsuit — for the hotel pools or a beach day.
  • Sunglasses and high-SPF sunscreen.
