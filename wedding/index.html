Taste of Portugal

What to eat & drink

You will not eat badly in Lisbon. But make sure these cross your table at least once.

Pastel de nata

Get one at Manteigaria and one at Pastéis de Belém — then argue about which is better.

Bacalhau

Portugal claims 100+ ways to cook its beloved salt cod. We won't judge your favorite. (Paul's photo — he ate this one.)

Grilled fish & seafood

Fresh off the boat, simply grilled. Ramiro is the legend — book ahead.

Bifanas & piri-piri chicken

Casual, cheap, incredible. Best eaten standing at a counter.

Ginjinha

Sour-cherry liqueur, a Lisbon ritual. Order it com — with a cherry in the cup.

Vinho Verde & Douro reds

Ask your waiter to pick. You genuinely cannot go wrong.
Timing tip: July is peak sardine season — follow the smoke to charcoal grills in Alfama's lanes.

Beyond the wedding week

Making a real trip of it?

If you're staying on after the wedding, southern Portugal is the classic move. These are multi-day trips — think two or three nights, not an afternoon — and they pair perfectly with a rental car and no particular hurry.

The Algarve is Portugal's southern coast: golden cliffs, warm water, and fishing towns. It's one easy motorway (A2) almost the whole way down from Lisbon.

Vilamoura

Resort town built around a huge marina — golf, beach clubs, and long dinners by the boats.

~2¾ hrs from Lisbon

Lagos

The postcard Algarve: golden cliffs, sea caves, and a lively old town. Ponta da Piedade is the showstopper.

~3 hrs from Lisbon

Albufeira

The liveliest beach town on the coast — big beaches by day, a buzzing old town at night.

~2½ hrs from Lisbon

Sagres

The dramatic southwestern tip of Portugal — a clifftop fortress, huge Atlantic views, end-of-the-world feeling.

~3¼ hrs from Lisbon

Further afield

Seville, SpainAdventurous

Already in the Algarve? Seville is only about 2 hours further by car across the border (A22 to A-49) — tapas, the Alcázar, and flamenco make a great two-day detour.

Tangier, MoroccoThe big one

The most ambitious add-on, for anyone making a real journey of it: drive on to Tarifa, Spain (about 4 hours past the Algarve), then it's a ~1-hour fast ferry across the strait to Tangier. Another continent before lunch — bring your passport and book the ferry ahead.

The practical stuff

Good to know

Everything your group chat was going to ask anyway.

Getting around

  • Uber and Bolt are everywhere, cheap, and the easiest way to move around.
  • Tram 15 runs out toward Belém; the classic 28 winds through Alfama.
  • The hotel shuttle can drop you in the center — ask the front desk for times.
  • Lisbon is hilly and cobbled — assume every walk has a climb in it.

Before you go

  • Currency is the euro. Cards are accepted almost everywhere; keep a little cash for kiosks and trams.
  • Outlets are European two-pin (types C/F), 230V — pack an adapter.
  • July is hot and sunny — high 80s°F days, breezy evenings by the river.
  • Tipping is modest here — rounding up or ~5–10% for great service is plenty.

Pack for this

  • Comfortable walking shoes — seriously, the cobblestones are no joke.
  • Formal, dressy attire for the wedding day.
  • A light layer for breezy evenings.
  • Swimsuit — for the hotel pools or a beach day.
  • Sunglasses and high-SPF sunscreen.
